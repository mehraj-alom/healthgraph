01 · The insight dial

One ring. Ten signals. Your call what it shows next.

Sleep, energy, water, stress, movement, wellbeing, pain, meals, heart, and doses each get a spoke, and each spoke sits inside its healthy band or steps outside it where you can see it happen.

02 · Food scan

Dinner first, arithmetic second.

Nobody keeps a food diary for long when every entry means typing ingredients into tiny boxes. So the diary works the other way here. You frame the plate, tap once, and about forty seconds later the meal is sitting in your log with calories and macros attached, ready for you to correct.

How a photo becomes a number you can trust enough to log

Vision AI names each food on the plate and judges portions against the plate itself, then the USDA nutrition database fills in calories, protein, carbs, and fat per hundred grams. Because a photo is evidence rather than measurement, the answer arrives as a range with a confidence note, and every item carries gram steppers so a heavy pour of rice takes two taps to fix. Logging works offline and syncs when you reconnect.

03 · Health chat

Ask the week what happened.

The assistant reads before it speaks. When you ask how sleep went, it has already pulled the week of sleep points, the energy curve beside it, and anything your records say that bears on the question. Answers arrive as streamed sentences with the receipts attached: quoted lines from your own documents and, where anatomy is involved, a button that jumps straight to the structure in the 3D body.

It also knows what it should not do. Doses and diagnoses are off limits, uncertainty is stated instead of smoothed over, and every reply ends with two or three follow-up questions it genuinely expects, which means most second questions cost two taps instead of a paragraph of typing.

Your sleep averaged 7h 10m this week, up from 6h 20m last week. Energy followed it upward on four of seven days.
Has my stress changed too?
Down a full point since Monday. The timeline has the daily entries if you want them.

A stop button under your thumb

Most health questions arrive in pairs, and the second one depends on the first answer. The chat keeps the thread and the sources visible, and a stop control stays on screen the whole time a reply generates, because an answer that looks wrong should be stoppable rather than watchable.

05 · Pulse check

Two ways to take a pulse, zero wearables required.

The fingertip method is the classic one: cover the camera, hold still, and watch the live waveform draw itself while the quality meter decides whether the reading counts. The face method needs nothing but light. It reads the pulse remotely from skin tone changes over about thirty seconds, which makes it the option you will actually use on an ordinary Tuesday.

Both end in the same place. Results carry a quality score and heart rate variability where the signal supports it, then land on the dial as the heart spoke and in the timeline as a dated entry. A bad reading is discarded with an explanation instead of being filed as fact.

The trace is the truth serum

Any app can print a number. The live waveform is what lets you believe it, because you can see a shaky hold ruin the signal in real time and fix your grip before the result commits. That is why the trace stays on screen the whole way through instead of hiding behind a spinner.

06 · Meds and symptom map

The treatment half of the record.

Schedules that survive real life

You define each medicine once with its dose, times, and days, and the day view reduces the rest to a single decision per row: taken or not. One tap stamps the time. Adherence becomes a spoke on the dial, which turns out to motivate more reliably than any reminder sentence, though the reminders stay.

A body you can point at

Thirty seven regions across front and back views, each tappable, each with a severity slider for the days a spot flares. A thirty day replay strip shows how each place behaved over the month, which is the difference between telling your doctor the shoulder hurts and showing them the four weeks it hurt.

Promises

What we refuse to do.

No invented precision

An estimate ships with its range and its confidence, or it does not ship. Missing days show as missing days. A blank is always more honest than a plausible number.

No medical cosplay

The app spots patterns and keeps records organized. It does not diagnose, does not prescribe, and says plainly when a question belongs with a clinician.

No data market

Your logs sync to your private account for backup and are never sold. Uploaded documents are processed only to build your timeline. Sign out keeps local data on the device.

No dead ends offline

Logging, meds, the dial, and history work without a connection and sync quietly when one returns. Anything needing the network says so instead of spinning.
