⚖️ Compliance map: what we can build without becoming a regulated company

Researched October 2026 · FDA (General Wellness guidance reissued Jan 2026, device-software policy, warning letters, 510(k)/De Novo database) · FTC enforcement (Flo, GoodRx, Premom, BetterHelp, Instant BP) · HHS/OCR FAQs · WA MHMDA · CCPA · GDPR/MDR · Apple + Google store policy. (?) = single-source, unverified.

The core rule — FDA's two-factor test (both required): (1) only a general-wellness intended use — fitness, nutrition, weight, sleep, stress, mindfulness, reproductive health in general terms, judged from labeling, ads, UI and functionality, not just the disclaimer; (2) low risk. Backed by the Cures Act: software “for maintaining or encouraging a healthy lifestyle and unrelated to diagnosis, cure, mitigation, prevention, or treatment” is not a device at all. Jan 2026 clarification: non-invasive sensing (PPG, accelerometer) may show values, ranges, trends, baselines — but any one of these flips you out: references to specific diseases or clinical thresholds · alerts prompting clinical action · treatment guidance (dosing) · “clinical-grade / medical-grade / as accurate as” claims · measuring or estimating physiology for screening, diagnosis, monitoring, or management.
Disclaimer truth, validated: disclaimers do not cure device intent. FDA's July 2025 warning letter to Whoop's BP estimation held that BP measurement is “inherently associated with diagnosis” and disclaimers do not cure — Whoop closed it only by changing the feature (June 2026). FTC barred Instant Blood Pressure ($596k) and melanoma-risk apps despite fine print; efficacy claims need real evidence, testimonials don't count. But disclaimers are still mandatory: Google Play requires the literal sentence “not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition” in the listing, and Apple requires reminding users to check with a doctor before medical decisions. So: disclaimers are necessary, never sufficient. The feature's function and claims do the real work.

Privacy posture, validated: as a direct-to-consumer app with no provider contracts we are generally outside HIPAA — and user-directed sharing (doctor PDF, caregiver report, FHIR pull) does not create Business-Associate status. The real exposure is elsewhere: FTC Health Breach Notification Rule (GoodRx $1.5M, Premom $100K, BetterHelp $7.8M — every case was sharing health data with ad/analytics SDKs against promises), Flo 2021 (period data to Facebook/Google analytics; consent order, no fine), WA My Health My Data Act (broad health-data definition, private right of action, geofence ban — on-device storage reduces but does not exempt; needs policy + consent + deletion flows), CCPA (Healthline $1.55M for purpose-limitation violations and trackers on health content), GDPR (health = special-category data: explicit consent + DPIA for large-scale processing; Clue's posture is the reference), EU MDR Rule 11 (decision-support software = Class IIa minimum — we stay out by never interpreting data for clinical decisions; note Clue's fertility algorithm is CE Class I, so even light predictions can qualify if framed as medical). Our shield is architectural: on-device data + zero third-party health-data sharing + affirmative consent before any share. No analytics SDK touching health content, ever.
Store gates (both mandatory): Apple (Mar 2026): medical-device transparency declaration per region (existing apps must declare by early 2027 or lose updates); dosage calculators only from approved entities; remind users to check with a doctor. Google Play: Health-apps declaration (done/required), the literal “not a medical device…” sentence in the listing, in-app privacy policy, prominent consent. Ban on sensor-only BP/temp/glucose/SpO2 claims on both stores.

❤️ Heart — 35%

vs Whoop (strain/recovery/sleep loop, $199–359/yr) · Oura (readiness, $70/yr + ring) · HRV4Training ($12 one-time) · Welltory ($100/yr)

Have

  • 30-day heart view + trailing/prior-month comparison
  • Health Connect import (HR, sleep, exercise, weight, steps)
  • Readiness hook fed by HRV + sleep inputs
  • Wellness-only positioning (no fake diagnoses)

Partial

  • HRV present as readiness input, no dedicated HRV trend/baseline card
  • Resting-HR visible in data, no RHR trend card or deviation alerts

Missing

  • HR zones + time-in-zone per workout (Strava/Whoop standard)
  • VO2max estimate
  • Strain / training-load score (acute vs chronic, fitness–fatigue–form)
  • Respiratory rate, SpO2, skin-temp, RHR-deviation illness heads-up
  • Behavior-impacts analysis (what moved HRV/RHR this week)
  • Exportable 30/180-day heart report for clinicians
  • Morning-measurement protocol (HRV4Training-style guided read)
P0: RHR trend card · HRV baseline card · HR zones · behavior impacts. Next: VO2max, clinician report.
Intentional-out: ECG/AFib detection, BP estimation, irregular-rhythm notifications — regulated medical-device claims. Never built without clearance. Proven by enforcement: FDA's July 2025 Whoop warning letter held BP estimation “inherently associated with diagnosis” and stated disclaimers do not cure device intent.
